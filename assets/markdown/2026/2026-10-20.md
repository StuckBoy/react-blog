# Web Dev, It *Just Works*
# (Until It Doesn't)
## 2026-10-20
To give some background, my web development experience certainly isn't 
"industry standard". There is also growing pains to learning something new. 
However, if my years on this planet have taught me anything, it's that the 
lizard-brain intuition I've developed over the years isn't random. To that end,
it finds numerous things weird about how modern web development works.

My years at university involved minor web development. If anything, it was web 
development in a time capsule. We used static HTML, CSS, and JQuery to perform 
basic operations. It was *rough*. However, when something was wrong, tracing the
problem back to its source wasn't difficult. Now compare that to the modern day 
tech stack for web development.

In my mind, there are only two ways the modern stack makes sense any sense to
me:
- The difficult work was done by someone else, who you do your best to imitate whenever designing something new
- You have a magic machine you can ask for the harder stuff (who was trained on the guy from the first bullet)

Call it cynical, but it's the vibe I've been feeling while trying to accomplish
what I would consider to be "simple" tasks for web development. For instance,
how long should it take to create a blog that dynamically loads entries into a
"Post" component? Apparently, longer than a few hours for a beginner. It makes
me wonder just what an enterprise-scale solution looks like under the hood.
If it's anything like learning about the environment your favorite car
manufacturer works in, you begin to wonder just how their cars stay together
while doing 70+ MPH on the road.

I'm taking a second to give modern web its flowers and highlight the things that
I *did* enjoy. If you only need simple styling on your components, being able to
place it directly within the HTML-within-the-Typescript is great. From my 
experience in a larger project, a CSS file can become a breeding ground of 
abandoned and duplicated stylings that only a sleuth could possibly untangle.
Unfortunately, this is also a case of a double-edged sword, because overreliance
on these types of in-line declarations can lead to a nightmare of a refactor as
you seek to update "unified" values. Like all things, a careful balance should 
be considered.
